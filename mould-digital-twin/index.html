Physics-Informed AI Digital Twin for Early Mould Prediction

A narrated walkthrough of the framework, the case study and the results: mould risk assessed surface by surface and predicted up to one week before it becomes visible.

What the video shows

Most building management systems watch energy and comfort, while dampness and mould develop unseen on or beneath building surfaces, and are usually dealt with only once visible or reported. This demo presents a digital-twin framework that detects where mould risk is developing and predicts how it will progress before that point.

An IoT sensor network measuring indoor, outdoor and envelope-surface conditions feeds a calibrated EnergyPlus model built from BIM, which estimates surface temperature and humidity at both monitored and unmonitored surfaces. The VTT mould growth model converts those conditions into a mould index, and a physics-informed XGBoost model (PIXGB), trained on features that encode hygrothermal and mould-growth physics, forecasts the index from one hour to one week ahead.

Validated over ten months in an occupied 1970s duplex at Syracuse University, the framework revealed glazing-adjacent surfaces and a low-insulation concrete wall as hidden hotspots even while room-air humidity stayed moderate, and outperformed both an LSTM and pure physics-based extrapolation. The closing section sets out what this means for housing providers, including under the UK's Awaab's Law.

  • R² ≈ 0.90one full week (168 h) ahead, with RMSE never above 0.028
