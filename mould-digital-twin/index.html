Physics-Informed AI-Integrated Digital Twin Framework for Early and Real-Time Prediction of Mould Growth in Buildings

Abstract

Indoor air quality issues, particularly mould and dampness, remain persistent public health concerns in residential buildings despite improvements in building standards and energy performance regulations. In current practice, mould management is largely reactive and relies on occupant complaints and visual inspection after mould has already become visible, at which occupants may have already experienced prolonged exposure to unhealthy indoor environment. To address this limitation, this study proposes a Digital Twin–based framework for continuous monitoring and predictive assessment of mould risk in buildings. The framework combines continuous environmental monitoring using an Internet of Things (IoT) sensor network with a calibrated Building Energy Model (BEM) that synchronizes real-time sensor data with simulation outputs to estimate surface hygrothermal condition across both monitored and unmonitored locations. These hygrothermal conditions are then used to evaluate mould development using the VTT mould growth model. Building upon this physics-based representation of mould dynamics, a Physics-Informed Machine Learning (PIML) model based on Extreme Gradient Boosting is developed to forecast mould growth across prediction horizons ranging from 1 hour to 1 week. The proposed framework was implemented and validated on a sensor-instrumented residential building case study located in Syracuse, New York, achieving high prediction accuracy of up to R² of 0.99 for short-term forecasts and approximately 0.90 for longer horizons up to one-week-ahead mould index prediction. The findings demonstrate reliable prediction of mould development under dynamically changing indoor environment. The framework provides a scalable approach for real-time monitoring and proactive mould risk assessment in new and existing building to improve operation and add a new automated layer for maintenance, enabling early smart decision-making and intervention.
