See mould coming before it shows

MouldCast tells residents and their landlord, a week ahead, where mould could grow on the walls of a home, why, and what may help.

It is the application layer of a PhD on AI-integrated digital twins for the early detection of damp and mould in UK social housing. Sensors in the home feed a digital twin, the VTT mould model turns each surface’s conditions into a mould index, and a forecast gives time to act before mould can be seen. This draft runs on a simulated flat, entirely in your browser.

How it works

From sensors to a week’s warning

  1. Sensors measureTemperature and humidity in every room and on the coldest walls, found by a thermal imaging survey, with the weather outside. No cameras, no microphones.
  2. The twin fills the gapsA building information model gives each wall its construction and material. The twin links every reading to a surface and estimates the surfaces that carry no sensor.
  3. A mould index for every surfaceThe VTT mould model turns how warm and damp a surface has been, and for how long, into an index from 0 to 6.
  4. A forecast, and whyA physics-informed model forecasts each index a week ahead, and explains which conditions drive it.
  5. Photos check itResidents photograph every monitored surface each week. Where the forecast and the photo disagree, the model is recalibrated.

The mould index

Acting while mould is still invisible

Mould grows in stages. Visual inspections and complaints only see it from stage 3, by which time a household may have lived with it for weeks. A forecast of the index finds the surfaces heading there first.

  • 0No growth
  • 1Small amounts of mould, seen only under a microscope
  • 2Several small colonies, seen only under a microscope
  • 3Visible mould, less than 10% of the surface
  • 4Visible mould, 10 to 50% of the surface
  • 5Visible mould, more than 50% of the surface

VTT mould index (Hukka and Viitanen, 1999; Ojanen et al., 2011).

About this draft

What is real and what is simulated

The VTT model, its material classes and the Awaab’s Law deadlines are real. The flat, its sensors and its weather are simulated, the forecast is the VTT model run on the expected conditions, and “what’s driving it” comes from re-running the twin without each factor. In the study, a physics-informed model trained on monitored homes replaces both, with SHAP explanations, and a large language model answers residents’ questions from the twin’s data.

The proof of concept is published in Building and Environment, with a video summary.
